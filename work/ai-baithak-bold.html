Bold palettes Q to V

Six new directions that stay away from both earlier sets. The first set (A to F) was black and orange with mustard, and the second (L to P) was soft pastels and greys. These use deep, saturated colors with a strong accent, picked so that real photos of people look good on them.

Deep, not pale

No tint-and-lighten shortcuts. Each palette has one rich dark base (petrol, oxblood, ultramarine, bottle green, graphite, cobalt) that carries the cover and gives photos a strong ground.

Built around skin

Every palette is checked against five skin tones on its dark, light and accent grounds. Cool and deep bases such as navy, petrol and oxblood sit well with warm skin. The strips show it.

Graphics get their own accent

One loud accent per palette for pills, shapes, numbers and charts, plus a quieter support color. Contrast ratios for the text pairs are listed so nothing ships unreadable.

What the research said

  • Neutral and deep backgrounds are the safest for people of all skin tones. Navy and earthy tones are named most often for fair, medium and deep complexions.
  • Pale, grey-green or yellow-green grounds can cast a color tint on faces, so greens here are kept deep and used for panels, not behind faces.
  • 2026 trend writing points to saturated colors with one loud accent, and advises keeping a stable core and letting the accent vary.
  • Many photo guides were tested mostly on lighter skin, so the strips here include deeper tones to check the covers do not wash them out.
