Six bold color options

A third direction, built to look nothing like the first two: A to F were black and orange with mustard, and L to P were soft pastels and greys. These are deep, saturated colors with one loud accent, picked so real photos of people look good on them. The lettering continues, so these are Q to V.

Each option shows six sample posts (three with people and the robot from your workshop post), story highlight covers and a nine-post feed, plus a skin-tone and contrast check. Photos keep their full color. Click any color to copy its hex code.

How the research shaped these

Deep, not pale

Each option has one rich dark base (petrol, oxblood, ultramarine, bottle green, graphite, cobalt). Photos sit on a strong ground, and the colors are not lightened versions of the earlier sets.

Built around skin

Neutral and deep backgrounds, and navy and earth tones, are the ones photographers name as the safest across skin tones. Greens are kept deep so they do not tint faces. The strips under each palette show five skin tones on its grounds.

One loud accent

2026 trend writing favors saturated color with a single strong accent on a stable core. Each option keeps that accent for pills, numbers and one highlight per post.

Contrast is checked

Every option lists the contrast ratios for its main text pairs. Where a pair is weak, the notes say which color to use for text instead.

My pick

Use Q · Petrol & Coral as the main look. Coral keeps some warmth from your orange, petrol is a skin-friendly deep ground, and it works for people, events and graphics.

For event hype and registration posts, add S · Ultramarine & Lime. For AI and tool content, U · Graphite & Neon has the most technical feel.

This is a recommendation. The final choice is the team's.
